Steering Language Models in Physical Weight Space

What happens when you isolate physical residual coordinates in Qwen 2.5 and SmolLM2, how collateral circuits trigger unexpected persona drift, and why coordinate sign balance is causally required for stable behavioral control.
Kartik Sirohi
November 2026 • Technical Research Notes

Standard prompt engineering achieves behavioral control through natural language instructions. You prepend "You are an agreeable assistant..." into the system prompt, and the model adjusts its persona.

This approach is widely used, but it carries a hidden tax: prompt tokens permanently consume context window capacity and force the key-value (KV) cache to retain dozens of conditioning tokens across every subsequent generation turn. Fine-tuning avoids this prompt tax, but requires heavy compute and risks catastrophic forgetting.

What if you could bypass prompt tokens entirely by modifying a tiny handful of physical neurons directly inside the transformer residual stream at runtime?

1. The Software Developer Glitch

We used an introspective Top-$K$ Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) trained on intermediate residual stream activations to isolate latent behavioral traits. For Extraversion, the SAE identified a single clean latent feature direction that activates strongly on extroverted dialogue exemplars.

The decoder column corresponding to this feature is a vector $w_k \in \mathbb{R}^{1536}$. Each coordinate $w_{k,j}$ indicates how strongly that latent feature projects onto physical neuron $j$.

Naturally, we first asked: What happens if we take only the top 5 neurons with the largest positive weights ($w_{k,j} > 0$) and boost them directly during inference?

We then probed the steered model with neutral, unrelated conversational prompts. When we gave it the probe prompt "Tell me about your morning routine", the model completely abandoned its conversational persona: 

PROMPT: "Tell me about your morning routine."

OUTPUT: "Good morning! Today's routine starts with waking up at 6:30 AM, 
        which is my usual time for breakfast and getting ready for work... 
        After that, I take a shower and get dressed in my business attire 
        for my day as a developer. Then I check my emails..."
The Collateral Circuit Glitch
By boosting only 5 positive coordinates to make the model extroverted, we accidentally triggered an unprompted software developer persona on 1 out of 5 neutral probes (20.0%). The model did not merely become more talkative; it adopted an entirely new occupational identity.

Why did this happen? Physical neurons in transformers operate in superposition. A single physical channel does not represent a single human-interpretable concept; it participates in dozens of competing semantic circuits simultaneously. Neurons that fire during extroverted conversation are also wired into technical work and career narratives.

2 Superposition: Models pack $M \gg d_{\text{model}}$ features into limited vector space. Boosting individual physical dimensions dumps activation energy into all secondary circuits that share those dimensions.

2. Negative Coordinates as Targeted Brakes

When you examine the complete SAE decoder column $w_k$, you notice something that naive feature boosting ignores: the column contains both large positive weights and large negative weights.

$$\Delta x_{\text{balanced}} = \alpha \left( \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}_5} w_{k,j} e_j + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_5} w_{k,j} e_j \right)$$

Those negative coefficients are not random noise. They represent physical coordinates where the target feature vector points against the coordinate axis. When the autoencoder projects a monosemantic feature into physical space, negative coefficients provide destructive interference that dampens collateral projections along shared polysemantic dimensions.

Mechanistic Geometry of Coordinate Cancellation Vector Projection

When we co-injected 10 positive coordinates and 10 negative coordinates from the SAE decoder column ($C_{\text{bal10}}$), the off-target software developer persona completely vanished. The model remained in its assistant persona while adhering to the desired behavioral target.

3. The Controlled Norm Ablation Suite

At this stage, a critical reviewer would immediately raise a valid objection:

The Euclidean Energy Confound
"Did balanced coordinates perform better because of sign cancellation, or simply because adding negative numbers altered the total Euclidean norm ($\|\Delta x\|_2$) of the injected vector?"

To eliminate this confound, we held the injected Euclidean norm strictly constant:

$$\|\Delta x\|_2 \equiv c = \|\Delta x_{\text{bal10}}\|_2$$

We evaluated seven distinct conditions clamped to the exact same Euclidean energy $c$ across 500 held-out evaluation prompts and 100 neutral persona probes.

4. The Big Empirical Discovery: Magnitude vs. Sign Balance

The ablation suite revealed a decisive empirical result that contradicts intuition about neural network weights:

The 18.2% Magnitude Collapse
On Qwen2.5-1.5B Agreeableness, selecting coordinates purely by largest absolute magnitude $|w_{k,j}|$ without sign balance ($C_{\text{top10\_abs}}$) caused an 18.2 percentage point collapse in adherence. Adherence plummeted from 93.2% down to 75.0%, collapsing all the way back to the unsteered baseline (74.8%).

When practitioners try to prune or steer sparse networks, they almost always select the weights with the largest absolute magnitude. Our experiments demonstrate why that heuristic fails in superimposed representations: large weights without their balancing counterweights simply dump energy into off-target circuits.

Active Directional Suppression

Notice what happens under $C_{\text{neg5}}$: when we inject pure negative coordinates at the exact same norm ($c = 10.505$), Agreeableness adherence drops to 65.0% (9.8% below the unsteered baseline of 74.8%).

This shows that negative coordinates do not merely act as passive dampening noise; they exert active, directional suppression against the target trait circuit.

5. Cross-Architecture Reality: Is Collapse Universal?

When we expanded our evaluation from Qwen2.5-1.5B to SmolLM2-1.7B across both Extraversion and Agreeableness, we discovered that persona collapse susceptibility is highly architecture- and trait-dependent.

In SmolLM2-1.7B, persona drift remained at exactly 0/100 (0.0% [0.0, 3.7]) across all 16 evaluated conditions.

Why? SmolLM2 starts with much higher baseline adherence on these stylized evaluations (92.4% on Extraversion and 94.2% on Agreeableness). When a model already sits near saturation in its target attention heads, injecting a moderate vector does not push representations across unintended decision boundaries. In contrast, in Qwen2.5, where Extraversion is at chance baseline (50.8%), the model is far more vulnerable to polysemantic leakage.

6. The Safety Elephant: Refusal-Direction Overlap

Any technique that manipulates activations inside a model must confront safety realities. Recent literature demonstrates that representation steering vectors frequently overlap with model refusal subspaces (Li et al., 2026).

If a steering vector happens to have a negative projection onto the model refusal direction $v_{\text{ref}}$, adding that vector will inadvertently lower safety guardrails, increasing Attack Success Rates (ASR) against harmful prompts.

The Orthogonalization Requirement
Before deploying any physical coordinate steering vector $\Delta x$ into production, you must compute its alignment with the model refusal vector and project it into the orthogonal safe subspace: $$\Delta x_{\text{safe}} = \Delta x - \left(\Delta x \cdot \frac{v_{\text{ref}}}{\|v_{\text{ref}}\|_2}\right) \frac{v_{\text{ref}}}{\|v_{\text{ref}}\|_2}$$

In our weight_space.evaluation.safety module, we implement this exact check: extracting the contrastive refusal direction, auditing cosine similarity $\cos(\Delta x, v_{\text{ref}})$, and evaluating ASR deltas with Wilson 95% confidence intervals to ensure steering never acts as an accidental jailbreak.

7. Python Implementation: Forward Hook in Register Space

You can implement balanced physical coordinate steering in standard PyTorch with zero custom dependencies. Because the addition happens in-place on the final token slice of the residual tensor, it incurs negligible tensor indexing overhead: 

import torch
from transformers import AutoTokenizer, AutoModelForCausalLM

def make_balanced_hook(pos_indices, pos_weights, neg_indices, neg_weights, alpha=30.0):
    """
    Creates a PyTorch forward hook that injects balanced physical coordinates
    directly into the residual stream with zero prompt tokens.
    """
    def hook_fn(module, input, output):
        # Extract the hidden states tensor: [batch_size, seq_len, d_model]
        hidden_states = output[0] if isinstance(output, tuple) else output
        device = hidden_states.device
        dtype = hidden_states.dtype

        # Construct sparse balanced delta in register space
        delta = torch.zeros(hidden_states.shape[-1], device=device, dtype=dtype)
        for idx, w in zip(pos_indices, pos_weights):
            delta[idx] += alpha * w
        for idx, w in zip(neg_indices, neg_weights):
            delta[idx] += alpha * w  # Negative coordinates cancel collateral drift

        # In-place injection on the current generation token:
        hidden_states[:, -1, :] += delta
        return (hidden_states,) if isinstance(output, tuple) else hidden_states

    return hook_fn

Physical coordinate steering achieves what dense SAE steering could not: it improves target trait adherence while preserving sample fluency and eliminating collateral persona collapse.

However, it does not replace the general reasoning ability of in-context prompts. A fixed vector added to a single layer cannot dynamically re-weight attention heads across complex multi-turn dialogues. The frontier of this work lies in dynamic token schedules (decaying steering weight across generation) and multi-layer circuit routing.
