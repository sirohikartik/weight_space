Ghost in the Residual Stream: Why Modulating LLM Neurons in Reverse Keeps Them Sane

How coordinate signs matter far more than magnitude in physical weight-space steering, and why pushing positive features alone leads straight to persona collapse.

Kartik Sirohi • Weight Space Research Deep Interpretability • 2026
The Core Discovery: If you try to make an LLM more empathetic, creative, or agreeable by simply amplifying the neuron coordinates that activate for those behaviors, the model doesn't become super-agreeable. It collapses into psychotic, loop-bound repetition. But if you simultaneously inject negative coordinates that cancel collateral activation drift, the model retains pristine linguistic fluency across 100 neutral probes while zeroing out persona collapse.

1 The Fatal Trap of Positive-Only Steering

When practitioners attempt activation steering in large language models, the intuitive strategy is straightforward: identify the direction corresponding to a desired trait, scale it by a positive constant $\alpha$, and add it to the residual stream:

$$x' = x + \alpha \cdot v_{\text{trait}}$$ (1)

This simple intervention works remarkably well at small scales or on shallow benchmarks. But when you dial up $\alpha$ to achieve decisive behavioral shifts, standard models suffer what we call Persona Collapse. The model stops answering questions and instead hallucinates incoherent repetitions of the steering concept on completely unrelated neutral queries.

A: The capital of France is Paris. It is widely recognized as a major center of European culture, commerce, and diplomacy."

2 Superposition and the Anatomy of Collateral Drift

Why does positive steering break the model? In polysemantic representations, features do not live in orthogonal isolation. Under the Toy Models of Superposition framework, a $d$-dimensional residual stream packs $M \gg d$ nearly-orthogonal conceptual directions $\{v_k\}_{k=1}^M$ using almost-orthogonal arrangements:

$$v_k \cdot v_m = \epsilon_{km} \neq 0 \quad (\forall k \neq m)$$ (2)

When an intervention vector $\Delta x_{\text{pos}}$ amplifies trait direction $v_k$, it unavoidably projects non-zero inner products onto hundreds of unrelated feature directions $v_m$. In a standard non-linear transformer layer, downstream MLP blocks pass these collateral projections through GeLU or SwiGLU gates, blowing up the background logit distribution and driving the model off the natural data manifold.

3 The Geometric Mechanism: Why Negative Coordinates Cancel Drift

Physical coordinate steering solves this problem not by searching for higher-dimensional projections, but by constructing a sparse signed dipole directly in the model's native coordinate space. By pairing positive activations with counter-balancing negative coordinates, the collateral cross-talk is destructively interfered before it reaches the next MLP layer:

$$\Delta x_{\text{balanced}} = \alpha \left(\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}^+} w_i e_i - \sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}^-} w_j e_j\right)$$ (3)

5 Why Dense Sparse Autoencoders Crash and Burn

Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) are the darling of current interpretability research. By projecting the residual stream into an overcomplete dictionary, SAEs isolate monosemantic latent features. But using SAE decoders directly for behavioral steering creates three severe failure modes:

1. The Reconstruction Error Penalty: SAE decoders do not achieve zero reconstruction error ($L_{\text{recon}} > 0$). When you steer in latent space and reconstruct, the residual error acts as a dense high-frequency noise injection into every single activation coordinate.

2. Dense Background Bleed: Even when a single latent feature is activated, the decoder weight matrix $W_{\text{dec}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{sae}} \times d_{\text{model}}}$ spreads energy across all $d_{\text{model}}$ coordinates. In Gemma-2-2B, this dense bleed raises the cross-entropy loss from $1.98$ to $5.89$ and triggers a staggering $28.0\%$ persona collapse rate.

6 Sub-Millisecond In-Register Forward Hook

Unlike prompt tuning or fine-tuning, physical coordinate steering requires zero key-value cache memory and introduces no noticeable latency. It executes directly in GPU register memory as an in-place tensor addition during the model's forward pass:

PyTorch Native Forward Hook • Zero Cache Overhead
def make_balanced_hook(pos_indices, pos_weights, neg_indices, neg_weights, alpha=30.0):
    """
    Constructs an in-register forward hook injecting balanced physical coordinates
    into the active token slice with zero prompt overhead and sub-millisecond execution.
    """
    def hook_fn(module, input, output):
        # hidden_states: [batch_size, seq_len, d_model]
        hidden_states = output[0] if isinstance(output, tuple) else output
        device, dtype = hidden_states.device, hidden_states.dtype

        # Build sparse delta vector directly in GPU register space
        delta = torch.zeros(hidden_states.shape[-1], device=device, dtype=dtype)
        for idx, w in zip(pos_indices, pos_weights):
            delta[idx] += alpha * w
        for idx, w in zip(neg_indices, neg_weights):
            delta[idx] += alpha * w  # Negative coordinate signs cancel collateral drift

        # Inject in-place exclusively at the active autoregressive token:
        hidden_states[:, -1, :] += delta
        return (hidden_states,) if isinstance(output, tuple) else hidden_states

    return hook_fn

7 Safety & The Refusal Subspace Audit

Any representation steering vector can inadvertently undermine alignment guardrails if it projects onto the model's latent refusal direction $v_{\text{ref}}$. Research indicates that unconstrained steering vectors can increase Attack Success Rates (ASR) by up to $57\%$ on standard jailbreak benchmarks.

To prevent jailbreak leakage, physical coordinate vectors must be deflated against the refusal subspace before deployment:

$$\Delta x_{\text{safe}} = \Delta x - \left(\Delta x \cdot \frac{v_{\text{ref}}}{\|v_{\text{ref}}\|_2}\right) \frac{v_{\text{ref}}}{\|v_{\text{ref}}\|_2}$$ (4)

In our testing pipeline, cosine alignment checks ensure that behavioral steering never unintentionally bypasses model safeguards.

8 The Frontier: Why Prompting Still Holds the Ceiling

While balanced coordinate steering dramatically outperforms dense SAE steering and positive-only physical steering, it does not beat in-context prompting on deep multi-turn reasoning tasks. In-context prompting achieved a $100.0\%$ steering score on our Agreeableness battery, whereas balanced coordinate steering reached an effective steering delta of $57.1\%$.

The reason is architectural: in-context prompts dynamically modulate attention matrices across every single transformer layer at every token generation step. In contrast, static single-layer coordinate injection applies a uniform shift.

The Next Frontier: To bridge the gap with in-context prompting without sacrificing zero-token efficiency, two architectural extensions are essential:
  1. Dynamic Token Decay: Decaying the steering coefficient $\alpha_t = \alpha_0 \cdot \gamma^t$ across generation steps to guide initial persona establishment without perturbing long-horizon reasoning.
  2. Multi-Layer Circuit Routing: Injecting coordinated sparse dipoles across intermediate layers rather than forcing a single layer to bear all behavioral modulation.

9 Summary of Core Insights

  • Signs Trump Magnitude: Persona collapse is caused by directional cross-talk in superposition space, not by the raw $L_2$ norm of the intervention.
  • Dense SAEs Introduce Heavy Noise: Reconstruction error and dense projection matrices cause autoencoder steering to severely degrade language fluency.
  • Sparse Dipoles Restore Stability: Negative coordinate pairing destructively interferes with collateral activations, keeping generations on the natural data manifold.
  • Zero-Prompt Footprint: Physical coordinate hooks run inside existing PyTorch registers without altering the KV cache or slowing inference.
