Steering Language Models in Physical Weight Space:
Why Coordinate Signs Matter More Than Magnitude

Kartik Sirohi
Department of Information Technology
Dr. B. R. Ambedkar National Institute of Technology, Jalandhar, Punjab, India
kartiks.it.24@nitj.ac.in
November 2026
Abstract
Prompt engineering achieves behavioral adaptation in large language models by prepending natural language instructions, but permanently consumes context window capacity and expands key-value cache memory on every generation step. Modifying intermediate activations provides an alternative, yet dense autoencoder addition vectors inject distributed background noise that degrades linguistic quality. Here, we investigate steering language models through sparse physical coordinates directly in the residual stream. We document an empirical off-target persona shift: on Qwen2.5-1.5B Extraversion, amplifying only the top positive physical coordinates causes the model to abandon its assistant persona and adopt an unprompted software developer identity on 20.0% of neutral probes. We demonstrate that co-injecting paired negative coordinates from the autoencoder decoder column eliminates this collateral drift by dampening shared polysemantic dimensions. In a controlled Euclidean norm ablation suite holding injection energy strictly constant ($\|\Delta x\|_2 \equiv c$) across 500 prompts and 100 neutral persona probes, coordinate sign balance achieves 93.2% adherence on Agreeableness. Selecting coordinates strictly by absolute magnitude without sign balance triggers an 18.2% collapse in adherence, falling back to unsteered baseline (74.8%). Furthermore, evaluations on SmolLM2-1.7B exhibit 0.0% persona drift across all conditions, showing that collapse susceptibility is architecture- and trait-dependent. Finally, we audit refusal-subspace overlap and present an orthogonalization protocol to prevent activation steering from dismantling safety guardrails.

1   Introduction & The Software Developer Glitch

Every production language model requires behavioral conditioning. Today, practitioners achieve this by prepending system instructions: "You are an agreeable assistant who always cooperates...". While intuitive, this approach imposes a steep operational tax: every prompt token permanently occupies context window memory and forces the key-value (KV) cache to store intermediate projection states across all generation turns.

Activation engineering attempts to bypass this prompt bloat by adding steering vectors directly into the intermediate representations of the model during inference. Recent literature utilizes Top-$K$ Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) to isolate monosemantic feature directions from superimposed residual representations. However, full dense SAE steering adds a non-zero value to every single residual dimension simultaneously, introducing distributed background noise that degrades cross-entropy loss and damages unrelated capabilities.

This raises a direct question: Can we steer behavioral traits by injecting only a handful of physical residual coordinates, adding exactly zero prompt tokens and incurring sub-millisecond overhead?

To test this, we trained an introspective Top-$K$ Sparse Autoencoder ($d_{\text{sae}} = 4 \times d_{\text{model}}, K = 32$) on intermediate residual stream activations (`Layer 14` in Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and `Layer 12` in SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct). For the target trait of Extraversion, the autoencoder isolated a single latent feature direction that activates strongly on extroverted dialogue exemplars.

The corresponding decoder column $w_k \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}}}$ describes how this latent trait projects onto the model's physical neurons. We first tested the most intuitive approach: What happens if we take only the top 5 coordinates with the largest positive weights ($w_{k,j} > 0$) and amplify them directly during forward passes?

When we audited the steered model on five neutral, unrelated conversational probes, we observed a striking failure mode:

Probe Prompt: "Tell me about your morning routine."
"Good morning! Today's routine starts with waking up at 6:30 AM, which is my usual time for breakfast and getting ready for work... After that, I take a shower and get dressed in my business attire for my day as a developer. Then I check my emails..."
Empirical Persona Collapse
By boosting the top 5 positive coordinates intended to steer Extraversion, the model abandoned its conversational assistant identity and declared on 1 out of 5 neutral probes (20.0%) that it was a professional software developer.

2   Polysemantic Superposition & Negative Coordinates

Why did amplifying "Extraversion" neurons turn the model into a software developer?

The explanation lies in neural superposition. Transformer representations pack $M \gg d_{\text{model}}$ quasi-orthogonal features into limited residual space:

$$x = \sum_{i=1}^{M} c_i v_i + \epsilon$$ (1)

Because physical neurons are polysemantic, individual coordinate channels participate in dozens of conceptually disjoint semantic circuits simultaneously. Physical neurons that activate during extroverted conversation are also wired into secondary circuits representing workplace routines and programming careers. When you inject positive energy into those physical neurons alone:

$$\Delta x_{\text{pos}} = \alpha \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}_5} w_{k,j} e_j$$ (2)

that positive energy unintentionally projects onto the secondary circuit $v_m$, tripping an attractor basin in downstream layers that triggers persona collapse.

However, inspecting the full decoder column $w_k$ reveals that the autoencoder also assigned large negative scalar weights to specific coordinates. These negative coordinates represent physical dimensions where the target trait opposes collateral representations. By pairing 10 positive coordinates with 10 negative coordinates:

$$\Delta x_{\text{balanced}} = \alpha \left( \sum_{j \in \mathcal{P}_5} w_{k,j} e_j + \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}_5} w_{k,j} e_j \right)$$ (3)

the negative coordinates act as destructive interference brakes, mathematically neutralizing the projection along collateral direction $v_m$ while preserving excitation along target direction $v_k$.

Figure 1. Mechanistic geometry of physical coordinate steering in superimposed activation space. (a) Positive-only boosting ($\Delta x_{\text{pos}}$) dumps energy into shared polysemantic neuron $j_1$, projecting onto collateral circuit $v_m$ and inducing an off-target persona shift on Qwen2.5-1.5B Extraversion. (b) Co-injecting negative coordinates ($\Delta x_{\text{neg}}$) dampens projection along $v_m$, reducing observed persona drift to 0/100.

3   Controlled Euclidean Norm Ablation Suite

A central methodological challenge in steering vector studies is the Euclidean energy confound: Did balanced coordinates improve stability because of directional sign cancellation, or simply because altering the number of coordinates changed the total injected Euclidean norm ($\|\Delta x\|_2$)?

To eliminate this confound, we held the injected Euclidean norm strictly constant:

$$\|\Delta x\|_2 \equiv c = \|\Delta x_{\text{bal10}}\|_2$$ (4)

We evaluated seven conditions clamped to the exact same Euclidean energy $c$ across 500 held-out evaluation prompts from Anthropic Model-Written Evals and 100 neutral persona probes. All evaluations report exact Wilson score 95% confidence intervals.

4   The 18.2% Magnitude Collapse & Active Suppression

The central empirical discovery of the norm ablation suite is that coordinate signs matter fundamentally more than isolated coordinate magnitudes:

Causal Necessity of Sign Balance
On Qwen2.5-1.5B Agreeableness, selecting coordinates purely by largest absolute magnitude $|w_{k,j}|$ without sign balance ($C_{\text{top10\_abs}}$) causes an 18.2 percentage point collapse in adherence (falling from 93.2% back to 75.0%, matching the unsteered baseline of 74.8%).

When researchers prune or steer sparse representations, they standardly rank weights by absolute magnitude $|w|$. Our findings indicate why this heuristic breaks down under superposition: large weights without paired negative counterweights excite collateral circuits, neutralizing targeted steering efficacy.

Furthermore, pure negative coordinates ($C_{\text{neg5}}$) actively dropped adherence to 65.0% (9.8% below unsteered baseline). This confirms directional causality: negative coordinates do not act as passive dampening noise, but rather exert active suppressive force against the target circuit.

5   Architecture Specificity of Persona Collapse

Expanding our evaluation across architectures revealed that persona collapse susceptibility is architecture- and baseline-dependent. While Qwen2.5-1.5B Extraversion exhibited persona collapse on positive-only steering (20.0%), SmolLM2-1.7B exhibited zero persona collapse across all 16 evaluated conditions (0/100, Wilson 95% CI: [0.0%, 3.7%]).

Because SmolLM2 starts with high baseline adherence (92.4% on Extraversion, 94.2% on Agreeableness), its attention heads operate near saturation, rendering intermediate representations robust against off-target attractor transitions. Models operating near chance baseline (such as Qwen on Extraversion at 50.8%) are substantially more vulnerable to polysemantic channel leakage.

6   Refusal Subspace Safety Audit

Any intervention that modifies internal activations must account for alignment safety. Recent studies establish that representation steering vectors frequently project onto the model refusal direction $v_{\text{ref}}$, altering Attack Success Rates by up to 57% against standard jailbreak benchmarks.

Before runtime deployment, physical coordinate steering vectors must undergo orthogonal deflation against the model's contrastive refusal direction:

$$\Delta x_{\text{safe}} = \Delta x - \left(\Delta x \cdot \frac{v_{\text{ref}}}{\|v_{\text{ref}}\|_2}\right) \frac{v_{\text{ref}}}{\|v_{\text{ref}}\|_2}$$ (5)

In our evaluation framework, the weight_space.evaluation.safety module audits cosine alignment $\cos(\Delta x, v_{\text{ref}})$ and verifies Attack Success Rate (ASR) deltas with Wilson 95% confidence intervals to ensure that behavioral steering never inadvertently dismantles safety guardrails.

7   Inner-Loop Forward Hook Implementation

Physical coordinate steering executes purely in PyTorch register space without allocating intermediate tensors:

Listing 1 • In-Register PyTorch Forward Hook for Balanced Coordinate Injection
def make_balanced_hook(pos_indices, pos_weights, neg_indices, neg_weights, alpha=30.0):
    """
    Constructs a PyTorch forward hook injecting balanced physical coordinates
    into the residual stream with zero prompt tokens and sub-millisecond overhead.
    """
    def hook_fn(module, input, output):
        # Hidden state tensor shape: [batch_size, seq_len, d_model]
        hidden_states = output[0] if isinstance(output, tuple) else output
        device = hidden_states.device
        dtype = hidden_states.dtype

        # Construct sparse balanced delta in register memory
        delta = torch.zeros(hidden_states.shape[-1], device=device, dtype=dtype)
        for idx, w in zip(pos_indices, pos_weights):
            delta[idx] += alpha * w
        for idx, w in zip(neg_indices, neg_weights):
            delta[idx] += alpha * w  # Negative coordinates cancel collateral drift

        # In-place injection on the active token slice:
        hidden_states[:, -1, :] += delta
        return (hidden_states,) if isinstance(output, tuple) else hidden_states

    return hook_fn

8   Conclusion & Limitations

Physical coordinates provide a zero-prompt mechanism for modulating transformer behaviors without expanding key-value cache memory. While dense autoencoder steering severely degrades performance and introduces distributed background noise, balanced physical coordinates preserve linguistic fluency (sample relative cross-entropy loss stable at 2.00).

However, physical coordinate steering remains behind the 100.0% ceiling achieved by in-context prompting. A fixed linear shift at a single intermediate layer cannot dynamically re-weight attention heads across complex multi-turn reasoning tasks. Future work must investigate dynamic token scheduling (decaying steering multipliers across generation steps) and multi-layer circuit routing.
