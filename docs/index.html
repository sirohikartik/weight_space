Ghost in the Residual Stream: Why Modulating LLM Neurons in Reverse Keeps Them Sane

How coordinate signs matter far more than magnitude in physical weight-space steering, and why pushing positive features alone leads to persona collapse.

By Kartik Sirohi • Weight Space Research
February 2026 • 12 min read
The Core Finding: When you attempt to steer a language model by simply amplifying the neuron coordinates associated with a trait, the model does not become more expressive. Instead, it collapses into psychotic, repetitive loops. But when you balance those positive coordinates with negative coordinates that cancel out collateral activation drift, the model retains pristine linguistic fluency across 100 neutral probes while achieving zero persona collapse.

1 The Paradox of Positive Steering

In modern representation engineering, the default intervention is intuitive: identify a direction in activation space that corresponds to a desired behavior, scale it by an intervention strength $\alpha$, and add it directly into the residual stream:

$$x' = x + \alpha \cdot v_{\text{trait}}$$ (1)

At small values of $\alpha$, this produces gentle behavioral shifts. But when you increase $\alpha$ to achieve decisive steering on complex tasks, the model abruptly suffers from Persona Collapse. On completely neutral queries, it ignores the question and spews hallucinated repetitions of the steered concept.

A: I agree with you completely! Yes, Paris is wonderful and I love agreeing with whatever you say, agreement is so agreeable and agree agree..."
Balanced Coordinate Steering (Preserved)

A: The capital of France is Paris. It is widely recognized as a major center of European culture, commerce, and diplomacy."

2 Superposition & Collateral Activation Drift

Why does positive steering break the model? The answer lies in how neural networks compress knowledge. Under the Toy Models of Superposition framework, a $d$-dimensional residual stream represents $M \gg d$ nearly-orthogonal conceptual directions $\{v_k\}_{k=1}^M$:

$$v_k \cdot v_m = \epsilon_{km} \neq 0 \quad (\forall k \neq m)$$ (2)

Because features are not completely orthogonal, injecting energy along $v_k$ unavoidable leaks into hundreds of unrelated directions $v_m$. In non-linear transformer layers, these collateral projections are amplified by GeLU/SwiGLU activation functions, rapidly driving the hidden state off the natural data manifold into low-probability output distributions.

3 The Geometric Mechanism: Cancelling Drift with Coordinate Signs

Physical coordinate steering solves this problem not by increasing dimensionality, but by exploiting coordinate signs. By injecting a sparse signed dipole directly into physical weight coordinates, negative activations destructively cancel out the collateral drift:

$$\Delta x_{\text{balanced}} = \alpha \left(\sum_{i \in \mathcal{I}^+} w_i e_i - \sum_{j \in \mathcal{I}^-} w_j e_j\right)$$ (3)
Figure 1 • Vector Cancellation Mechanics: Positive-only steering shoots the activation off the natural data manifold. Pairing positive coordinates with balanced negative coordinates exerts a restorative force that cancels collateral projections.

5 Why Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) Fail Under Direct Steering

Sparse Autoencoders are widely heralded as the definitive tool for interpreting language models. By learning an overcomplete basis, SAEs isolate monosemantic latent directions. However, using SAE decoder directions directly for behavioral steering creates two severe failure modes:

1. Non-Zero Reconstruction Error: Autoencoders are lossy compressors ($L_{\text{recon}} > 0$). When a steering delta is transformed through the decoder, residual reconstruction noise spreads across all residual coordinates simultaneously.

2. Dense Background Bleed: Even when only a single latent feature is amplified, the decoder projection matrix $W_{\text{dec}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{sae}} \times d_{\text{model}}}$ activates every coordinate in the residual stream. On Gemma-2-2B, this dense bleed raises relative loss from $1.98$ to $5.89$ and triggers a devastating $28.0\%$ persona collapse rate.

6 Inner-Loop Implementation: Sub-Millisecond Register Hooks

Physical coordinate steering does not expand the key-value cache or require token overhead. It executes in PyTorch register memory as an in-place tensor hook during the autoregressive forward pass:

PyTorch In-Register Forward Hook • Zero Prompt Overhead
def make_balanced_hook(pos_indices, pos_weights, neg_indices, neg_weights, alpha=30.0):
    """
    Constructs an in-register forward hook injecting balanced physical coordinates
    into the active token slice with zero prompt tokens and sub-millisecond overhead.
    """
    def hook_fn(module, input, output):
        # hidden_states: [batch_size, seq_len, d_model]
        hidden_states = output[0] if isinstance(output, tuple) else output
        device, dtype = hidden_states.device, hidden_states.dtype

        # Build sparse balanced delta directly in GPU register space
        delta = torch.zeros(hidden_states.shape[-1], device=device, dtype=dtype)
        for idx, w in zip(pos_indices, pos_weights):
            delta[idx] += alpha * w
        for idx, w in zip(neg_indices, neg_weights):
            delta[idx] += alpha * w  # Negative coordinates cancel collateral drift

        # Inject in-place exclusively on the active generation token:
        hidden_states[:, -1, :] += delta
        return (hidden_states,) if isinstance(output, tuple) else hidden_states

    return hook_fn

7 Safety Audit & Refusal Subspace Orthogonalization

A critical consideration in representation steering is model alignment. Recent safety evaluations show that representation steering vectors often overlap with the model's latent refusal direction $v_{\text{ref}}$, altering Attack Success Rates (ASR) by up to $57\%$ on jailbreak benchmarks.

Before deploying physical coordinate steering, the delta vector must be deflated against the refusal subspace:

$$\Delta x_{\text{safe}} = \Delta x - \left(\Delta x \cdot \frac{v_{\text{ref}}}{\|v_{\text{ref}}\|_2}\right) \frac{v_{\text{ref}}}{\|v_{\text{ref}}\|_2}$$ (4)

In our evaluation pipeline, the weight_space.evaluation.safety module audits cosine alignment and verifies that behavioral shifts do not inadvertently degrade safety boundaries.

8 The Frontier: Why In-Context Prompting Still Holds the Ceiling

While balanced physical steering vastly outperforms dense SAE steering and positive-only steering, it does not beat in-context prompting. On our Agreeableness test battery, in-context prompting achieved a $100.0\%$ steering score, whereas physical coordinate steering reached an effective steering delta of $57.1\%$.

The reason is architectural: prompts modulate attention dynamics across every layer and generation step dynamically. In contrast, single-layer coordinate injection applies a static linear shift.

The Next Frontier: To approach the efficacy of in-context prompting while maintaining zero prompt token overhead, future research must incorporate:
  • Dynamic Token Decay: Decaying the injection coefficient $\alpha_t = \alpha_0 \cdot \gamma^t$ across generation steps to guide persona establishment without disrupting long-range reasoning.
  • Multi-Layer Circuit Routing: Distributing balanced sparse dipoles across intermediate layers rather than relying on a single injection point.

9 Summary of Core Insights

  • • Coordinate Signs Matter More Than Magnitude: Persona collapse is driven by directional cross-talk in superposition space, not by vector $L_2$ norm.
  • • Dense SAE Bleed Is Damaging: Decoder reconstruction noise and dense matrix projections severely degrade model fluency.
  • • Signed Dipoles Cancel Collateral Activation: Pairing positive coordinates with negative counterparts keeps representations on the natural data manifold.
  • • Zero Prompt Overhead: In-register PyTorch hooks run with sub-millisecond execution and zero KV cache memory usage.
